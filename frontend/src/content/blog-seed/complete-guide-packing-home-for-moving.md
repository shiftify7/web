Packing fails when cartons mix rooms and weights. Pack one room at a time, keep dense things in small boxes, and label as if a stranger will unload the truck.

## Quick take

Finish spare rooms first. Leave the kitchen and the bed you still sleep in until the end. Number cartons. Keep an essentials box that opens first in the new house.

## Pack by room

A “miscellaneous” carton from three rooms is how you lose the kettle for two days.

A workable order:

1. Spare bedroom and storage
2. Books and décor
3. Clothes you will not wear this week
4. Kitchen last
5. Bathrooms the night before

If a crew is packing for [house shifting](/services/house-shifting/), the same order still helps you live in the house until loading morning.

## Materials that are enough

You need fewer products than shops will sell you.

- Double-wall cartons for books and kitchen
- Smaller cartons for anything dense
- Paper or foam for glass
- Stretch wrap for drawers you cannot empty
- Tape that still sticks in humidity

If you cannot lift the carton to waist height, split it. Books in a large carton crush the bottom and the person carrying it.

## Books, clothes, kitchen

**Books.** Spine-down or flat. Mix paperbacks with a hardback so the carton is not a brick. Label the destination room.

**Clothes.** Wardrobe cartons for hanging workwear if you have them. Fold the rest. Do not pack damp laundry.

**Kitchen.** Empty the fridge a day before. Plates travel on edge with paper between them. Glasses get their own carton, filled so they cannot rattle. Photograph appliance cables. Empty water from filters.

## Fragile items and electronics

One category per carton. Do not put a blender on wine glasses because there is a gap. Fill voids so nothing shifts when the box tilts.

Mark fragile on two sides and the top. Tell the supervisor which cartons they are.

Laptops, hard drives and cameras travel with you. Original boxes help for screens; otherwise wrap face-in and use a carton that does not flex.

## Furniture

Empty drawers. Tape glass shelves separately. Bag bed bolts and tape them to a slat, labelled. Do not oil wood the day before — stretch wrap will slide.

## Labels and inventory

Write the **new** house room name, the main contents, and fragile if true. “Guest room” in the old flat may be “study” in the new one.

Number cartons: Kitchen 1 of 8. The last number tells you if something is missing before the truck leaves.

A simple sheet — number, room, contents — plus a photo of the sheet is enough.

> Essentials box: kettle or one pan, mugs and cutlery for a meal, soap, towels, chargers, medicines, bed sheet. Hand it to the supervisor as last off, or keep it in the car.

## Mistakes that cost an evening

- Liquids without a bag
- One giant mixed carton
- Cash or jewellery “hidden” in a drawer
- Tape so thick you need a knife that scratches the contents
- A wet fridge sealed for the road

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I pack or should the crew?

Either works if it is written on the quote. Mixing “you pack the kitchen, we pack the rest” without saying so is how kitchens get packed twice.

### How do I pack glass?

Vertical plates, dedicated glass carton, no heavy items on top, label two sides. Cheap newspaper on surfaces you care about can stain; use paper or foam.

### What opens first at the new house?

Essentials, beds, kitchen, bathrooms. Children’s own carton early, labelled with a name, not “kids misc”.
